A calm, clinical system for ophthalmology training

Two audiences, one vocabulary. Program directors need signal in ten seconds, while residents study on a phone between clinics. Both get the same palette, the same type scale and the same Material 3 components, tuned so that the admin side reads precise and the student side reads encouraging.

00 — Overview

Principles

Four rules, in priority order. Most design arguments on this product turn out to be a disagreement about which one applies, so when two options both look defensible, the one that better satisfies the earlier principle wins.

01

Signal before decoration

Every screen exists to answer one question, and whatever answers it gets the largest type on the page. Chrome, borders and icons stay quiet underneath. When nobody can say what decision an element helps someone make, that element goes.

02

Clinical, not sterile

Medical content earns precision: tabular numerals, honest charts, no rounding that flatters. None of that has to read as cold. Warmth comes from spacing, from roundness and from the way things are written, never from decorative illustration.

03

Two tones, one system

Directors get density and tables, because they are auditing a cohort rather than exploring one, while students get room to breathe and visible progress. What stays constant underneath is the token layer. If the two experiences ever seem to need a second visual language, something has gone wrong further upstream.

04

Legible under pressure

This gets read on a phone, in a dim lecture hall, over hospital wifi and often in a second language, which is why the floor is 14px body text, AA contrast throughout, and a visible state for every degraded condition rather than a blank screen.

00 — Overview

Audiences & tone

The same component can wear two voices. What changes between them is density, wording and how much the interface celebrates; what never changes is the colour, type and spacing underneath.

Admins & program directors

Simple and direct. They are auditing cohorts, not exploring.

  • Density: compact, with 44px rows and 13.5px body.
  • Surfaces: tables and stat rows over cards.
  • Copy: nouns and numbers. "12 residents below 60%."
  • Colour: neutral by default; colour only marks status.
  • Zero celebration. No confetti, no streaks, no badges.

Residents & students (incl. international)

Friendly, but clean and unambiguous. English is the platform language.

  • Density: comfortable, with 56px targets and 15px body.
  • Surfaces: cards, one primary action per screen.
  • Copy: short second person. Avoid idioms and phrasal verbs, which don't translate.
  • Colour: emerald for progress and correctness, never for decoration.
  • Praise is factual: "8 of 10 correct", not "Amazing!!".
translate
Writing for non-native readers Keep sentences under 20 words, one idea each. Never encode meaning in colour alone or in an icon alone — AI-assisted translations re-flow text, so labels must survive a 35% length increase without breaking layout.
00 — Overview

Borrowed vs. ours

Quizlet is the right reference for the student side: it makes studying feel light without being childish, and it never buries the next action. Copying its look, though, would hand an ophthalmology program a consumer-flashcard identity that nobody could defend in front of an accreditation board. What follows is where the borrowing stops.

check_circleAdopted
  • Mode-selection tiles. Large tinted panels that let a resident pick how to study instead of dropping them into one linear flow.
  • Generous radii. 16px cards, 24px tiles, pill CTAs on the student side. Softness does the friendliness so the copy doesn't have to.
  • A white card floating on a colour field. Great for previewing content inside a category.
  • Big confident headings with a lot of air above them.
  • One obvious next action per screen, always visible.
cancelDeliberately excluded
  • The reference palette. No indigo/yellow/peach quartet. EyeLecture tiles are low-saturation tints of the brand blue, emerald, amber and violet. The accent lives in the tint, not in a saturated block.
  • Saturated panels with white text. Ours stay tinted with dark text, which keeps AA contrast and reads clinical rather than consumer.
  • Illustrations and mascots. Medical content carries its own imagery: fundus photos, OCT scans. Decorative art competes with it.
  • Streaks, points, confetti. Motivation here is a passing threshold and a transcript, not a game loop.
  • Any of this on the admin side. Directors get density and tables. The warmth stops at the student boundary.
01 — Foundations

Colour

The old app's blue was a bright, high-saturation web-2.0 gradient, and the green sat directly on top of it at the same visual weight, so neither of them could carry meaning. The identity was worth keeping; the execution was not. The blue drops in luminance until white text passes AA on it, and the green moves from a mint tone to a deeper emerald so that it can mean something ("correct", "on track") rather than just fill space.

Desktop

12-column, 24px gutters, max content width 1280px. Side nav 236–256px fixed. Content never exceeds 1024px for reading views.

Mobile

Single column, 16px margins, 8px between cards. Bottom tab bar 56px + safe area. Minimum touch target 44×44.

Density

Two densities, switched at the layout level rather than per component. Note that this is a specification, not something the CSS currently implements — see Known drift.
Comfortable (student, mobile) uses 48px controls and 20px card padding. Compact (director tables, reports) uses 40px controls, 44px rows and 16px padding.

Shape & elevation

Rounder than the old app and rounder than stock Material, which is where most of the Quizlet-adjacent friendliness actually comes from. 01 — Foundations

Motion

Motion explains where something came from and confirms that a tap landed. It is never decoration on its own. That said, a system that only fades things in feels cheap, so the surfaces people actually touch get real physics: a spring on lift and press, a decelerating curve on arrival.

Durations

120ms · fast

Hover tint, focus ring, checkbox, button press. ease-standard

220ms · base

Card and tile lift, menus, chips, tabs, theme switch. ease-spring

360ms · slow

Entrance, dialogs, sheets, bars filling. ease-emphasized

520ms · flip

Flashcard reveal only. The single longest thing in the product.

Curves

ease-standard

(.2,0,0,1) — no overshoot. Anything that changes colour or opacity in place.

ease-emphasized

(.16,1,.3,1) — strong deceleration. Things that arrive: entrances, bars filling, sheets.

ease-spring

(.34,1.4,.5,1) — slight overshoot. Direct-manipulation surfaces: lift, press, pop. Never on text.

The four interactions worth animating (hover to preview)

Lift

Card hover

−4px on a spring, shadow to elevation 3, border warms to primary. Signals "this whole surface is one target".

Press

Button press

Scale to 96.5% in 120ms. Confirms the tap before the network does.

Entrance

Cards, tiles and bars fade up 18px with a 55ms stagger inside their group, once, the first time they enter the viewport. Progress bars grow from zero to their value on the same trigger, so a student watches the bar fill rather than finding it already full. Nothing replays on scroll back.

check_circleDo

Animate transforms and opacity only. Both run on the compositor, so they hold 60fps on a five-year-old hospital laptop.

cancelDon't

Don't animate a number counting up in a director's dashboard, don't loop an animation, and don't make anyone wait on motion before they can read a result.

Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion: transitions collapse to ~0ms, the shimmer skeleton becomes a static block, entrances resolve immediately and progress bars appear at their final width.

01 — Foundations

Accessibility

Non-negotiable, and partly regulatory, since transcripts and completion records have to be readable by anyone who asks for them. The target is WCAG 2.2 AA.

Contrast — measured, not estimated

  • on-surface on surface — 17.9:1
  • on-surface-variant on surface — 7.5:1
  • on-surface-muted on surface — 4.5:1 (labels only, never body)
  • white on primary #1B4FD1 — 6.8:1
  • white on secondary #0A7D4C — 5.2:1
  • Emerald 500 #0FA968 is 3.1:1 on white, so it is a graphic colour (bars, fills) and never carries text. Text-bearing green is always 600+.
  • on-primary-container on primary-container — 10.6:1
  • Dark theme: on-surface 14.6:1, primary 7.4:1, secondary 9.0:1

Beyond contrast

  • Focus ring is a 3px 55%-opacity primary halo, never removed.
  • Correctness is shown by colour and an icon and the word.
  • Touch targets ≥ 44×44; 8px minimum between targets.
  • Every data bar has an accessible text equivalent next to it.
  • Interface is fully operable at 200% zoom and 320px width.

03 — Patterns

Activity tiles

The student's entry point: four ways into the same lecture. Each tile is a tinted panel with a white preview card inside it, so the content of a mode is visible before anyone commits to it. This is the one place in the system where colour is decorative, and it is capped at four tints, one per activity, fixed across the whole product so that the colour ends up working as a wayfinding cue rather than as noise.

  • Tile-to-activity colour mapping is fixed: blue Watch · emerald Practise · amber Flashcards · violet Review. Never reassign it per lecture.
  • Tiles are the only decorative use of colour in the product. Everywhere else, colour means status.
  • Each tile states its size ("18 questions", "42 min"), because students choose by how much time they have rather than by curiosity.
  • Tiles never appear in the director experience.

05 — Handoff

Tokens

Three layers. Product code only ever reads layer 2 or 3, which is what makes dark mode and any future rebrand a value swap instead of a refactor. The block below is exactly what powers this page, hardcoded exceptions included.

/* Layer 1 — reference (never used directly in components) */
--el-blue-600: #1B4FD1;   /* brand primary */
--el-green-500: #0FA968;  /* brand secondary */
--el-n-900: #0F172A;      /* text */

/* Layer 2 — Material 3 system roles (what components consume) */
--md-sys-color-primary: var(--el-blue-600);
--md-sys-color-on-primary: #FFFFFF;
--md-sys-color-primary-container: var(--el-blue-50);
--md-sys-color-secondary: var(--el-green-600);
--md-sys-color-surface: #FFFFFF;
--md-sys-color-on-surface: var(--el-n-900);
--md-sys-color-on-surface-variant: var(--el-n-600);
--md-sys-color-outline-variant: var(--el-n-200);
--md-sys-shape-corner-md: 10px;
--md-sys-shape-corner-lg: 14px;
--md-sys-elevation-1: 0 1px 2px rgba(15,23,42,.06);

/* Layer 3 — component tokens (only when a component genuinely differs) */
--qcard-answer-correct: var(--viz-correct);
--qcard-answer-chosen: var(--viz-4);
--table-row-height: 44px;

Angular Material / MUI

All names follow the MD3 --md-sys-* spec, so both libraries can be themed by overriding these variables rather than writing component CSS.

Figma

Layer 1 mirrors as a colour primitives collection, layer 2 as a semantic collection with Light/Dark modes. Components bind to layer 2 only.

Offline & email

Self-host both fonts and the icon subset. Emails use a hex-literal export of these tokens, since CSS variables aren't reliable in mail clients.

05 — Handoff

Known drift

A design system with no drift is a design system nobody has built anything with. What follows is the gap between what this document claims and what the CSS on this page actually does. It was found by auditing the file, not by reading it, and it is here so that the next person does not have to rediscover it.

The type scale is aspirational

Medium

The scale on this page documents whole-pixel steps, but the stylesheet ships 13.5px in eight places, 12.5px in eight, 11.5px in four and 15.5px in three. The half-pixel sizes crept in while fitting dense table rows and never got reconciled. Either the scale gains those steps or the components round to the nearest documented value; nobody has decided which.

Two hardcoded colours

Medium

#8AA8F5 on the snackbar action and #A8E8CB on the hero highlight are literals rather than tokens, which directly contradicts the claim made in Semantic roles. Both exist because the surface underneath them inverts between themes and the role layer still has no inverse-primary equivalent.

Layer-1 leakage

Medium

Five component rules read --el-* reference tokens directly: the hero gradient, the email header and the eye mark among them. The stated rule is layer 2 only. Closing this properly means adding role tokens for brand gradients, which is a bigger change than it looks and has been deferred twice.

Density is documented but not built

High

Spacing & layout describes a Comfortable and a Compact density switched at the layout level. No attribute or class implements it. Every screen on this page hardcodes its own sizes, which is exactly the divergence the density rule was written to prevent.

--md-sys-color-warning is invented

Low

Material 3 has no warning role. This one behaves like a proper role and the system genuinely needs it, but sitting under the md-sys prefix it implies a spec compliance it does not have. Renaming it to --el-sys-color-warning would be honest and would break every consumer, so it stays for now.

500 inline styles in this document

Low

They are documentation scaffolding rather than product code, so the severity is low. The risk is that 127 of them set a raw font-size, and anyone who lifts markup straight off this page inherits values that are not on the scale. Specimens should be moved into real classes before this doc gets used as a copy-paste source.

What's next

This release is enough to validate direction and to build the two screens above. Everything below is tracked debt, in suggested resolution order.

Settled — decided and documented

  • Emerald is meaning, not brand furniture: it marks correctness and completion only. Promoting green to an equal brand colour would change the semantics of every status in the system.
  • Violet reserved for AI-generated content, given the localisation flow.
  • Cards are bordered rather than shadowed, which is the biggest single move away from stock Material.
  • Persistent field labels instead of Material's floating labels, for translation safety.

Open — outstanding design debt

  • The eye mark here is a first pass and needs a proper lockup (horizontal, stacked, favicon, monochrome) before it ships.
  • Charts beyond bars: score distributions and trend lines over blocks.
  • The full question-authoring flow, including AI draft review states.
  • Print/PDF stylesheet for transcripts and cohort reports.
  • Whether alumni and med students share the resident IA or need their own.
